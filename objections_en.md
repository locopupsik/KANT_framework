# IV. Possible Counterarguments

1. **The Accusation of AI Paralysis**  
   A machine that takes over our thinking and decision-making is no longer a tool, but a dictator. If the price of seamless efficiency is the disempowerment of humanity, it is not worth it. Our constitution is not designed to fail quickly, but to triumph slowly and in a controlled manner. Paralysis is not a bug; it is a protective barrier.

2. **The Suicide Race**  
   Winning a race whose finish line is uncontrolled self-annihilation is irrational. If another nation decides to tear down all safety barriers and unleash an uncontrolled superintelligence, it unleashes an unpredictable optimizer that respects no national borders. To throw oneself into a reckless suicide race merely out of fear that the other side might pull the trigger first is no longer geopolitics—it is a guaranteed death blow. Historical analogies offer only limited guidance here, because an AGI differs fundamentally from previous inventions such as the hydrogen bomb: not only in its potential power, but above all in its goal-directedness.

3. **The Crisis Argument**  
   Climate change and pandemics are not problems of scientific understanding—they are problems of political and social coordination. If we delegate our political decisions to a “black box” AI in order to “save time,” we have ceased to be a democracy. We are capitulating before our own tasks. We do not need an all-powerful AI to solve crises; we need the courage to take responsibility as human beings.
